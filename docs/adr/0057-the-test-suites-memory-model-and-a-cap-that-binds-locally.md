# ADR-0057 — The test suite's memory model, and a cap that binds locally and not on CI

**Status:** Accepted — 2026-08-17 (issue [#114](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/miolos/issues/114), absorbing [#110](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/miolos/issues/110), shipped in #117)
**Depends on:** [ADR-0017](./0017-vitest-and-fast-check-are-the-test-stack.md), [ADR-0023](./0023-proved-not-sampled-property-testing.md), [ADR-0055](./0055-test-timeouts-are-sized-against-contention.md)
**Amends:** [ADR-0055](./0055-test-timeouts-are-sized-against-contention.md) — decision 1's *"the **`hookTimeout` axis is unswept**"* clause and its population figure, decision 2's *"contended"* procedure, decision 4's instantiated cost-driver list, and **two** of the Consequences bullets, with a **third adjudicated in place rather than amended**. The reciprocal `**Amended by:**` header is on that file, and it is the authority on the split: (a)–(f) decision-level, (g)–(h) the two Consequences made false, (i) the Consequence adjudicated and left standing.
**Annotates, and does not amend:** [ADR-0017](./0017-vitest-and-fast-check-are-the-test-stack.md) — its consequence *"Turbo fans out `pnpm test`"* stays true and becomes incomplete. No `**Amended by:**` header is owed there, and ADR-0055's own bullet about clearing ADR-0017 as untouched is adjudicated in place rather than left silent.

**The reference box changed while this ticket was open, and the diagnosis is scoped to that rather than quietly re-fitted.** The developer box's WSL2 allocation was raised from **15 545 MB RAM / 4 GB swap to 23 552 MB / 24 GB** (`.wslconfig`: `memory=24GB`, `swap=24GB`, `autoMemoryReclaim=gradual`, `processors=8`) after every decision below had been drafted against the smaller one. **Decision 1's exhaustion is a property of the 15.5 GB box and no longer reproduces**: on the enlarged box the suite is green at every fan-out including uncapped, with zero swap touched. Decision 2's cap survives the change, but on **different evidence** — it is now retained because it is the fastest and lightest setting, not because the machine runs out of memory without it — and its own text says so. Every figure in plan 049 §2.1–2.6 was taken on the old box; the re-measurement is that plan's §2.7. This paragraph exists because a decision whose stated cause has evaporated, left unannotated, is how a cap outlives its reason.

**The measurements are not here.** ADR-0055's Consequences say it in the terms this ADR obeys: *"An ADR carrying a measurements appendix is the fastest-rotting document in the repo … What this ADR keeps is the qualitative finding it rests on."* The RSS samples, the concurrency sweep, the five-boot phase split and the CI regression arithmetic live in [`docs/plans/049-issue-114-plan-the-fan-out-cap-and-the-pglite-hook.md`](../plans/049-issue-114-plan-the-fan-out-cap-and-the-pglite-hook.md) §2 and in #114's pull-request body. Every figure this ADR would otherwise state is one of: a fleet constant that moves with vitest, PGlite and Node; a sample maximum drawn from a heavy-tailed scheduling distribution; or a wall clock on one developer box. None of the three survives a year in an ADR.

## Context

Root `pnpm test` is `turbo run test`, and until this ticket it ran at turbo's default fan-out: six package suites at once, each `vitest run` at `maxWorkers = max(cpus − 1, 1)`. [#107](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/miolos/issues/107) fixed the `testTimeout` half of the resulting flake class and recorded ADR-0055; the half it could not reach was the PGlite `beforeAll` boot, which ADR-0055 decision 1 declared unswept because vitest prints no hook durations in any reporter. Handoff 048 left #107 open on exactly that: ten runs on `main`, nine reds, and the hook axis under all of them. That is #114.

The suite's PGlite population is one shape repeated: **26 test files across `packages/db`'s and `apps/api`'s suites call `createTestDb` in a top-level `beforeAll`** — 18 in `apps/api`, 8 in `packages/db` — each with an explicit `30_000` ms budget and an `await ctx.close()` teardown. Every `beforeAll` in the repo is one of those boots. The population's per-file census, including nine byte-distinct spellings of the same cost comment across those 26 sites, is in plan 049 §4.

Two constraints bind and are not re-opened. **ADR-0023** floors the `packages/games` run counts; nothing here touches that package. **ADR-0017** permits a `vitest.config.ts` only where the defaults do not suffice, and after this ADR's decision 2 they do.

## Decision

1. **The failure was memory exhaustion on the 15 545 MB box, not CPU oversubscription — and the box has since grown out of it.** Peak demand across a root run scales as **packages running concurrently × workers per package × resident set per worker**, and the middle term is `cpus − 1` for every package independently. On the reference developer box six packages at once demand materially more RAM than the machine has; what follows is swap thrash, and swap thrash is what turns a one-second WASM boot into a fifty-second one. The scheduler cannot: the CPU oversubscription across the concurrent boots is under 2× (plan 049 §2.2), and no amount of queueing converts a second into a minute.

   **The model's shape is recorded here; its constant is not.** The per-worker term is a fleet average over six heterogeneous suites and a PGlite-bearing worker is heavier than that average, so the model is an estimate of the demand curve and never a per-worker bound. Decision 2 therefore rests on direct whole-run samples rather than on the constant. Figures: plan 049 §2.2.

   **The model held; the constraint it ran into did not.** Raising the box to 23 552 MB left the demand curve exactly where this decision puts it — the enlarged box still measures roughly 17.7 GB at full fan-out against roughly 9.7 GB at the cap, the same ratio — but 17.7 GB now fits, so **no swap is touched, no boot is starved, and the hook class does not fire at any fan-out**. The mechanism is not refuted; it is unloaded. What survives unchanged is the *ordering* the model predicts, and that ordering is what decision 2 now rests on.

   **What did not change is the oversubscription.** Six packages at `cpus − 1` still put up to 42 processes on 8 cores, and that is why full fan-out remains the **slowest** configuration on the enlarged box despite having 6 GB to spare. Adding RAM removed the symptom and left the arithmetic; a reader who takes *"the memory problem is solved"* away from this ADR will re-raise the fan-out and get a slower suite.

   **What the boot actually costs, qualitatively.** PGlite's WASM `initdb` is the overwhelming majority of `createTestDb`; the migration replay is a rounding error beside it. Comments across the population that attributed the cost to *"real-migration replay"* or *"migration replay"* were over-weighting a component worth a few per cent, and they are replaced by one block at the cost driver (ADR-0055 decision 1 as amended here).

2. **The root `test` script caps turbo at 2, inline on the script, and the cap stays overridable.** `"test": "TURBO_CONCURRENCY=2 turbo run test"`. **Two is simultaneously the fastest and the lightest setting measured, and on the enlarged box it is the only one of those two claims that still does any work.** Nine runs on the enlarged box — three each at 2, 4 and 10, all green, no swap touched at any level — put two at **40–41 s** against four's 50–53 s and ten's 53–55 s, and at roughly **9.7 GB** against 12.8 and 17.7. One is slower than two by two thirds and buys nothing, because a single package still spawns `cpus − 1` workers, so serialising the packages does not reduce the floor.

   **The warrant is now speed and boot headroom rather than exhaustion, and the substitution is deliberate.** On the old box the argument was that the alternative did not complete; on this one every alternative completes and two is simply better on both axes anyone measures. That is a weaker warrant than the one this ADR was drafted with, and it is stated as the weaker thing it is rather than propped up with the superseded figures.

   **The placement is the decision, not the number.** `turbo.json`'s `concurrency` key is **global** in turbo's schema — it would cap `pnpm typecheck` silently and breaks `pnpm dev` outright below 3, because `dev` is persistent-task-bound. A `--concurrency=2` baked into the script is **not** overridable: turbo's CLI flag beats the env var, and passing `--concurrency` twice is a hard error, so a caller could not get above the cap at all. An **inline env assignment** is the one form a caller's flag can win over, which is what keeps CI (decision 3) able to run uncapped through the same script. An ambient-honouring `${TURBO_CONCURRENCY:-2}` form is rejected for a second reason beyond being unmeasured: the standing `TURBO_CONCURRENCY=1` prefix that agent sessions in this repo exported for months would keep binding, invisibly. The inline assignment ends that.

   `VAR=value cmd` in an npm script is POSIX-shell syntax. This repo develops on WSL2 and its CI is `ubuntu-latest`, so it is safe today; a Windows contributor is the trigger to revisit, not a reason to add `cross-env` now.

   **What the cap throttles is every task in the `test` run's graph, not only the six suites.** `turbo.json`'s `test` task declares `dependsOn: ["^build"]`, so on a cold cache a root `pnpm test` schedules the upstream builds through the same concurrency budget and they run two at a time as well. That is stated once because it is a consequence of where the cap sits rather than a defect — the builds are part of the same memory demand — and because "six package suites" is the phrase this ADR otherwise uses throughout, which a reader could take as the whole graph.

   **The residual, stated inside the decision because that is where it belongs.** *The cap bounds packages, not workers.* `maxWorkers` is still `cpus − 1` per package, so on a box with more cores per gigabyte the same cap demands more than this box's does, and **a higher cores-to-RAM ratio re-opens #114**. The shape the next ticket takes if that happens is named so it is not re-derived: a `packages/db/vitest.config.ts` declaring `poolOptions.maxForks`, which tightens the bound to a constant independent of core count. It is not shipped now because it would put an unexplained number in a new config file to solve a problem no box this repo has run on has had, and because ADR-0017 says a vitest config exists only where the defaults do not suffice.

3. **The cap does not bind on CI. CI stays the one runner that executes the suite at full fan-out, and it is now instrumented.** The gate passes `--concurrency=10` explicitly — which is turbo's own default, so the step is behaviourally identical to the bare `pnpm test` that ran there before this ADR: it **pins** the existing configuration rather than introducing one.

   **The reason is that there is nothing on that runner for the cap to relieve.** On the standard hosted runner's small vCPU count, vitest's own `maxWorkers` collapses to 1, so six packages demand a small fraction of the runner's RAM and the whole mechanism decision 1 describes cannot engage. Capping there would instead serialise six lanes into two and cost the gate step roughly half again its wall clock — the arithmetic, with the gate run it is derived from, is in plan 049 §2 and in the workflow's own comment. The runner's shape is printed on every gate run (`nproc && free -m`) so that this paragraph is checkable rather than asserted.

   **What diverges and what does not.** Pre-commit and CI stop running byte-identical commands, deliberately. **The enforced gate does not diverge**: the `gate` status context required by the `protect-main` ruleset still runs `pnpm test` over all six packages at fan-out 10. Only the advisory local hook is capped.

   **Two residuals, inside the decision.** *A runner upgrade re-opens #114 on the gate* — a larger-runner label, or the repo going public and landing on a bigger free class, multiplies `cpus − 1` where nothing is capped; the printed `nproc` line is the tripwire. And *CI's contended hook cost had never been measured at all*; decision 4 is what fixes that, and #114's own gate run is the first reading this population has ever had.

4. **The boot instrument ships, and it is turned on at the gate.** `createTestDb` reports its own phase split — WASM boot, drizzle wiring, migration replay, total — on stderr, behind `MIOLOS_TEST_DB_TIMING` and inert without it. **The enabling value is exactly `1`**: the instrument compares the variable against the string `"1"`, so `true`, `on` and `yes` leave it silently inert and a reader who sets one of those gets a clean, empty, entirely misleading run. The exact value is stated here, in the instrument's own doc comment and in the workflow's comment, because a silently inert diagnostic is worse than an absent one. It reads its caller off the stack rather than importing `vitest`, because it lives in `packages/db`'s `src` and is consumed across a package boundary through the `@miolos/db/testing` subpath. It is declared in `turbo.json`'s `test` task under `env`, without which turbo's strict env filtering drops it and the instrument is silently inert — the trap that cost this ticket's first diagnostic round.

   **This is what discharges ADR-0055 decision 1's "unswept" clause for this population**, and what gives it the CI-first anchor decision 2 of that ADR asks for and could not previously obtain. The clause is narrowed rather than deleted: every *other* hook in the repo is still unswept, for the unchanged reason that vitest prints no hook durations.

   **The first such reading exists**, and its existence rather than its value is what belongs here. #114's own gate run executed the suite uncapped with the flag on, printed its `nproc && free -m` line — which is decision 3's premise about the runner's shape, measured rather than assumed — reported a boot for every file in the population, and produced a pooled maximum comfortably under decision 5's tripwire. It also settles the open question of whether `turbo.json`'s `env` declaration suffices on CI: it does, with no `--env-mode=loose`. **The number is in #114's pull-request body and in plan 049 §2, and deliberately not here**, on ADR-0055's own *"the measurements do not live here"* — a first CI sample of a heavy-tailed distribution on a runner class GitHub can change without telling anyone is the exact figure that ages worst in an ADR.

   The cost is stated rather than discovered: every gate log grows by a stderr line per boot, permanently, and the flag joins the task's cache key so toggling it invalidates. On CI its value is a constant, so gate-to-gate caching is unaffected.

5. **The hook budget stays 30 000 ms, and the class is avoided by the cap rather than closed.** Those words, deliberately: a reader who takes *"fixed"* away from this ADR will size the next budget wrong. At default fan-out the class is uncovered — on a developer typing `turbo run test` directly, on any future revert of the cap, and in principle on CI, where the cap deliberately does not bind. **On CI it has not in fact fired**: on that runner decision 1's mechanism cannot engage, and #114's own gate reading is far under the ceiling. The local uncapped run is where it fires today, and a runner upgrade is what would move it onto the gate.

   **An eligible LOCAL anchor now exists, and on the old box it did not. This is the claim the box change falsified outright.** ADR-0055 decision 2 sizes from the highest measured figure, CI first and worst contended local second; an isolated run is never eligible, and a run in which anything timed out is never eligible either. On the enlarged box, **six uncapped local runs at the shipped 30 000 ms wall completed green with nothing timed out** — 8 597.1, 9 017.7 and 9 146.4 ms before the merge, then 8 085.7, 8 429.1 and 10 283.1 ms on `main` after it. Uncapped is the most contended configuration this repo has locally, so those are exactly what the procedure asks for. Run it: **10 283.1 × 4 = 41 132.4 → 45 000 ms**. CI now has two gate readings — 8 686.4 ms and 9 733.8 ms — and decision 2 takes the highest, giving **9 733.8 × 4 = 38 935.2 → 40 000 ms**. Decision 2 prefers CI, so the procedure names **40 000**. **Both halves land above the shipped 30 000, and they disagree with each other by one step**, which is itself why this is left below as a tension rather than as a number. Note also the drift *within* the local half: three further runs moved its maximum from 9 146.4 to 10 283.1 ms. That is a heavy-tailed sample behaving normally, and it is the concrete argument against ever moving this budget on a single reading.

   **The two disqualifiers below applied on the 15.5 GB box and are retained as a record of what was tested, not as live reasoning.** Neither was ever "every uncapped run was red" — several were not.

   *First, every uncapped local run taken at the shipped 30 000 ms wall had something time out* — the PGlite hook class in most of them, a `Test timed out` in the one where the class did not fire and a boot instead finished roughly six hundred milliseconds under the ceiling. Decision 2 disqualifies all of them on the right-censoring argument it already makes.

   *Second, every uncapped local run that stayed green did so only because the hook wall had been lifted* — to 60 000 or 600 000 ms, for diagnosis. Those runs are genuinely green, uncapped, and complete: every file boots and nothing times out. They are still not anchors, because they were taken under **a configuration this repo does not ship** and one ADR-0055 decision 2 does not contemplate — that decision varies contention and holds the shipped budgets fixed, and lifting a wall is the other thing. **The exclusion is argued rather than assumed because it decides the number**: the largest boot in that green set is 26 076.1 ms, and decision 2's ×4 with its round-up would put the budget at **105 000 ms**. An anchor drawn from a wall the repo does not ship would triple a shipped ceiling, which is precisely why the eligibility rule exists.

   The capped figures are not anchors either, by that ADR's procedure as amended here; they show only that the shipped ceiling is not breached under the shipped local configuration.

   **The CI half was already eligible, and the honest statement is uncomfortable in the same direction: both halves now agree against the shipped number.** The gate figure is green, uncapped, nothing timed out, and a real `cache miss, executing` rather than a replay — precisely the CI-first reading decision 2 prefers. Run decision 2 on it and the procedure does not say "keep the shipped number"; it says **the budget should be one step higher than it is**, and the enlarged box's local anchor says one step higher again. The arithmetic is in the PR body and in the comment beside `createTestDb`, where it belongs.

   **The number is nevertheless retained, on decision 4 rather than decision 2.** Decision 2 sizes a budget that is being set; decision 4 governs *moving* one already shipped, and it requires a diagnosed defect and fresh figures rather than an arithmetic result. No defect exists — this hook has not fired once under the shipped cap or on the gate. Raising it would spend real hang detection on **the largest hook population in this repo carrying an explicit budget, and in fact the only one** (the 75 `beforeEach` hooks are more numerous and every one rides the bare default) to buy a ceiling the local evidence says would not be a bound either, since a boot was measured at 98 % of the current wall without firing. `CLAUDE.md`'s mechanical gate forbids weakening a gate that does not need weakening. Lowering it is equally unwarranted, for decision 4's converse reason.

   **This is recorded as a tension rather than resolved away, and the box change widened it rather than closing it.** A ticket that re-derives the budget from a pooled sample across several gate runs *and* several uncapped local runs — rather than from single readings on either side — is the shape that settles it, and it should say which of decision 2 and decision 4 governs a ceiling whose own procedure has now drifted away from it on both axes at once. Stating that here is the point: the next session should find the disagreement, not a tidy number.

   **The tripwire, and the configuration it is read under**, so the next session does not re-argue it: take the pooled maximum from a **green** run — capped locally, or a CI gate log, which is uncapped with the instrument on. Over 40 % of the budget (ADR-0055 decision 1) the explicit in-file timeout this hook carries is confirmed as required. **Nothing currently fires it**: the capped local maximum is 3 033.5 ms (**10.1 %**), CI's pooled maximum over two gate runs is 9 733.8 ms (**32.4 %**), and the uncapped local maximum over six runs is 10 283.1 ms (**34.3 %**). The trigger is therefore untripped on every configuration measured, which is a reading of "no action", not of "confirmed". Over `budget / 2` (decision 4) it is a defect to diagnose and record, and only then, if it is drift rather than a draw, the cause to move the number with fresh figures in the pull-request body.

   **And a guard on the CI half of that, because the instrument is cacheable and its output is indistinguishable from a measurement.** Turbo caches a task's stdout and replays it byte-for-byte on a hit — the cached artefact for `@miolos/db:test` is literally that package's captured `test` log — so a replayed run reprints the instrument's lines complete with the pids and epoch stamps of the run that produced them. The gate restores `.turbo/cache`, and the `test` hash keys on each package's own inputs, so **the common case for a future pull request is that it does not touch `packages/db` and replays that package's timing lines unchanged**. Nothing in the line itself says so. **A CI figure counts only when the run's `@miolos/db:test` line reads `cache miss, executing` or `cache bypass`** — never `cache hit, replaying logs`; the epoch stamp, which will belong to an older run, is the secondary tell. #114's own gate reading is a genuine execution: the new `env` entry changed the hash.

   **The residual, inside this decision.** *The teardown half of the same population is unmeasured.* Every `afterAll` in the repo is one of these boots' `await ctx.close()`, and neither those nor any `beforeEach`/`afterEach` in the repo carries an explicit budget — all of them ride vitest's bare `hookTimeout` default, and the instrument reports nothing at close. A `close_ms` field beside the existing report is roughly three lines whenever someone wants it. This ticket neither measures that population nor makes it more expensive.

**A documented finding, recorded as a finding and explicitly not as a mechanism.** **A hook killed at the wall does not stop its boot.** The boot runs to completion regardless, so a failed file's process lingers roughly twenty seconds past its own failure, still inside `initdb`, while vitest has already scheduled further files against the same machine. That overlap is real and is part of why the wall partly causes its own failure — lifting the wall at identical concurrency produced runs in which every boot completed rather than being killed mid-`initdb`, and the hook class vanished from them. It did not make the boots cheap: the largest in a green lifted-wall run came within a few seconds of the shipped ceiling, and the lifted-wall runs that went red on `apps/web` tests carried boots past it.

**It is not a cascade a teardown could fix, and this ADR says so because an earlier draft of this ticket claimed the opposite.** Two checks, both run rather than argued (plan 049 §2.4): across every timed-out suite in five red runs, **not one** `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'close')` fired — the context was assigned before `afterAll` ran and every close succeeded — and vitest was confirmed to report both errors when that failure does happen, so the absence is real. And **`isolate: true` gives every test file its own process**: an abandoned PGlite instance dies with its own file and cannot accumulate across a run. Closing at `afterAll` happens *after* the boot has completed, which is precisely when the cost has already been paid; the lingering work is not reachable by anything a test file can run.

**The `isolate: true` dependency is recorded beside the finding, because two conclusions rest on it** — this one, and the boot semaphore's dismissal below. A future move to `isolate: false`, in any package, invalidates both and re-opens the teardown question this ADR closes.

6. **The worker count is bounded too, by a constant rather than by core count — and this is the decision that actually fixes #114.** `vitest.shared.ts` at the repo root exports `Math.max(1, Math.min(4, os.cpus().length - 1))`, and every workspace permitted a config imports it. Decisions 2 and 5 above bound the OUTER axis and left the inner one tied to `cpus − 1`; **that residual is not theoretical and was never theoretical** — it is the axis that made a developer machine unusable for days, and it was mis-scoped as a "future box" concern in decision 2's own text.

   **What it buys, measured on 8 cores / 23.5 GB.** The root `pnpm test` goes from 9 626 MB / 40 s to **6 670 MB / 39 s** — three gigabytes lighter and marginally faster. More importantly it binds where the turbo cap cannot: at `--concurrency=10`, which is what CI passes and what a bare `turbo run test` does, peak goes from 17 748 MB to **11 629 MB**. **A bound that only works when invoked through one script is not a bound**, and that is what shipped first.

   **`min(4, cpus − 1)` and not a flat 4, because the formula is what keeps CI unchanged.** On the two-vCPU hosted runner it yields 1, byte-identical to the behaviour before the file existed — checked against the `pool=1` reported by all 26 boots in gate run 32081091260, which is why that reading mattered. A flat 4 would have quadrupled the gate's workers on a runner with 7.9 GB.

   **`packages/games` is excluded, and the exclusion is enforced by a test.** ADR-0017 forbids a config there because importing `vitest/config` pulls vite's `.d.ts`, which references `@types/node`, into a package whose tsconfig sets `types: []` — silently defeating the typecheck backstop for the project's central invariant. `T-WEB-S229` now scans for its absence, and for `apps/web` keeping its jsdom, plugin-react and setup-file settings. **The scan exists because the first attempt at this decision broke both**: a glob generated a config for every workspace, which clobbered `apps/web`'s real config and put the forbidden import into `packages/games`. Neither failure was loud — the jsdom loss would have silently run component tests in the wrong environment.

   **What this changes about the box.** The `.wslconfig` raised to 24 GB during this ticket was an emergency measure against the unbounded default and is no longer warranted. Measured by holding the remaining RAM to simulate a smaller machine, the capped suite touches **no swap at any size tested** — floors of 9 675 MB at a simulated 16 GB, 5 976 at 12, 4 120 at 10, 2 487 at 8. **The normal path would fit in 8 GB.** The allocation is set to **16 GB** for a different reason: the *uncapped* path still peaks at 11 629 MB, so 12 GB would put a bare `turbo run test` back against the ceiling while 16 absorbs it, and the agent sessions that run beside the suite need room the suite's own figures do not show.

   **A first draft of this paragraph said 12 GB swapped 2 587 MB and was the original failure returning. That was false, and it was false because of a measurement error worth recording**: the simulation held memory with a background process whose kill was not verified, so three of them accumulated and the later runs were constrained far below their labels. **A simulation that does not assert its own preconditions is not a measurement**, and the same session had already been burned once by trusting a glob it did not verify.

## Rejected

- **A teardown registry in `packages/db/src/testing.ts` — a module-scope set of live instances and a `closeTestDbs()` as the only teardown path, with all 26 `afterAll` bodies rewritten.** Proposed, built as a plan, and dropped on measurement. The two defects it claimed to fix do not occur (see the finding above), the mechanism it would have addressed is not reachable from a test file, and it would have been a **net regression**: PGlite's `close()` blocks on the instance becoming ready, so in the uncapped worlds this ADR deliberately preserves, a hook killed at the wall could hand the teardown the remainder of a boot — replacing an error that never fires with a second, real hook timeout on hooks that carry no explicit budget at all. Two facts measured while testing it are kept even though the decision is gone: `close()` on a still-booting instance settles cleanly with no unhandled rejection, and a double or concurrent close throws, so any future batching teardown must clear its collection *before* awaiting.
- **A boot semaphore inside `createTestDb`.** A per-process no-op. Every vitest worker is its own process, and within one process the boots are already serial — one file at a time per worker, one top-level `beforeAll` per file. It would bound nothing across the processes that matter, and the thing it aims at is a confound anyway: forcing the two PGlite-bearing suites to run together at full workers, with nothing else on the box, was green and fast at the same concurrent boot count the red runs had.
- **`--maxWorkers` on the package `test` scripts — the inner axis.** On the 15.5 GB box it measured red: the run that tried it still exhausted memory and still timed out. **On the enlarged box it measures as the best memory result available, and it is still rejected — for a different and better reason.** `--concurrency=2 -- --maxWorkers=4` runs green at **6 781 MB** against the shipped cap's 9 626 MB, for about a second of wall clock; `--concurrency=4 -- --maxWorkers=2` gives 7 312 MB. The reason to decline roughly 2.9 GB of headroom is that **the root `test` script is shared with CI, and there the flag inverts**: the hosted runner's small vCPU count already collapses vitest to one worker, so a hard-coded `--maxWorkers=4` would *raise* the gate's worker count several-fold on the one machine where nothing is capped — spending decision 3's entire premise to buy headroom on a box that has 14 GB spare. Making it local-only needs conditional machinery in a manifest that cannot carry a comment. The residual inside decision 2 already names the shape this takes if a future box needs it: `poolOptions.maxForks` in a `packages/db/vitest.config.ts`, which binds where the boots actually are and not on the shared script.
- **`turbo.json`'s top-level `concurrency` key.** Global, breaks `pnpm dev` below 3, silently caps `pnpm typecheck`. Decision 2.
- **A percentage cap (`--concurrency=50%`).** Expresses the cap in the one term the diagnosis says is not the constraint. The binding resource is memory; a percentage of cores tracks the wrong axis and would drift on exactly the machine change decision 2's residual names.
- **Capping at 3 or at 4.** Three is measurably slower than two and demands materially more memory. **Four now has the memory sample it previously lacked, and the sample removes the only argument that had been in its favour:** on the enlarged box four measures 11 687–12 842 MB and **50–53 s** against two's 9 626–9 756 MB and **40–41 s**, so four is both heavier and slower. The earlier revision's claim that four was the faster setting came from the 15.5 GB box and does not survive re-measurement — it is corrected here rather than dropped, because it was the one figure that made this a close call.
- **Raising the hook budget instead of capping.** Measured, twice over: at double the budget and default fan-out the PGlite hook class disappeared entirely — every file booted, nothing hit a hook wall — and the suite still went red in two runs of three, each time on four `apps/web` tests across three files, with the same two (`T-WEB-S119` and `T-WEB-S203`) red in both. **The failure relocates; it does not close.** Those web reds are starvation victims of decision 1's exhaustion, not defects in the tests they landed on, and per ADR-0055 decision 2 no figure from those runs sizes anything.
- **Re-sizing any `testTimeout` here.** The tests that went red under the raised-budget runs were measured under a configuration this ADR does not ship, in runs where something timed out. Sizing against them would be sizing against #114.

## Consequences

(a) **Pre-commit and CI stop running byte-identical commands, and the divergence is the point.** It is recorded in three places that a reader actually reaches — this ADR, the workflow's own comment, and a fail-closed source scan in `apps/web/test/` that reds if either the cap or the CI override is dropped — because a divergence that lives only in a commit message is a divergence nobody knows about. The manifests it guards are JSON and carry no comments, so those scans **are** the comment `package.json` and `turbo.json` cannot have.

(b) **ADR-0055 is amended in its decisions 1, 2 and 4 and in its Consequences, and the edits are enumerated as a lettered mapping in that file's `**Amended by:**` header rather than summarised as a number** — a count is precisely what rots, and #114's own plan proved it by claiming three amendments and listing four. Two of its Consequences were made outright false by this ADR — *"The root fan-out is deliberately left uncapped"* and the PGlite carve-out that called this class *"a `hookTimeout` failure this decision never measured"* — and a third, the one asserting ADR-0017 and ADR-0023 are cited without amendment, is adjudicated in place rather than cleared silently, since ADR-0017 is annotated here. **That adjudication carries its own letter, (i), rather than riding inside (h)'s parenthetical** — which is the rule (d) states one entry earlier, applied to the bullet whose own subject is the failure of clearing a record wrongly.

(c) **ADR-0055 decision 2's local anchor procedure is executable again, and the box change is what restored it.** On the 15.5 GB box the procedure named a command — the uncapped local run — that could not be got to complete, which left **CI gate logs as the only practical anchor source**; that was a live constraint rather than a theoretical one, because [#109](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/miolos/issues/109)'s third residual is defined on the local axis, which no CI sweep can see. On the enlarged box the uncapped run completes green and the local axis is measurable again, so #109's residual is unblocked on this axis. **The constraint's return is a machine property, not a repo property**: it comes back the moment anyone runs this suite on a box with the old cores-to-RAM ratio, and nothing in the tree records what that ratio was. That is the tripwire this bullet leaves behind.

(d) **A budget sized against capped local figures under-shoots the worlds that are still uncapped.** Anything sized from a local run after this ADR is sized against two lanes, while CI, a bare `turbo run test` and any revert of the cap run six.
